PAGE 10: Digital Governance, Justice & Social Progress (#177 – #195)
============================================================

As cities expand digital surveillance networks, citizens rightly demand stronger legal safeguards for personal privacy and effective tools to counter online misinformation. Rapid workplace automation and every social-media algorithm now shape daily news consumption, driving waves of technological innovation alongside faster hardware obsolescence. Law enforcement agencies must also combat sophisticated cybercrime while addressing youth challenges such as juvenile offending and minor delinquency. Modern justice systems increasingly prioritise offender rehabilitation over harsh retribution, recognising that education and job training reduce repeat offences. In many countries, schooling is compulsory until late adolescence, whereas community mentorship programmes remain voluntary. When local councils provide sufficient funding rather than inadequate budgets, youth centres play a crucial role in building feasible career pathways and delivering remarkable social outcomes.
